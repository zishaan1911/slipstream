SX-01 power electronics, simulated in SPICE

Three circuits from the SX-01 power chain, built component by component and simulated in ngspice 45.2: the pack and power distribution bus, one ESC's MOSFET bridge driving its brushless motor, and the 500 kHz buck converter that feeds the companion computer. Each result is checked against the simpler model the flight simulator uses, and the differences were fed back into it.

Fed back into SlipStream

Motor. The flight model drove each motor as a plain 60 mΩ resistance against back-EMF. In the switching simulation, commutation and winding inductance make the motor behave like a larger resistance that grows with speed. Fitted over three speeds it is R_eff = 31 mΩ + 99.4 µΩ·s × ω, with an effective back-EMF constant of 7.07 mV·s/rad at 15° timing advance. The flight model now uses that, which lowers the top-end thrust of each rotor from 10.23 N to 7.52 N.

12 V rail. The flight model treated the regulator as a fixed 0.8 V dropout, so it predicted brownout at 11.8 V. The switching simulation shows the 95 % maximum duty and the converter's own losses set the limit instead. The rail model is now that ceiling minus a loss term fitted to the SPICE brownout point, v12 = min(12, 0.95·Vbus − 0.18 V). The fixed-dropout guess of 11.8 V turns out close; the larger change is that the rail starts sagging at 12.32 V rather than 12.8 V.

Pack, harness and ESC bus

battery_bus.cir
  • The surge pulls 176 A from the pack. On a 20 % pack the bus bottoms out at 11.20 V and settles near 11.32 V. A full pack holds 12.84 V. The flight model's resistive estimate (11.59 V) is close, because the pack's ohmic resistance dominates.
  • On a 20 % pack that surge would reset the companion computer. The bus falls below the 11.77 V where the 12 V rail drops out (see the buck section). This is why the autopilot climbs at a limited rate and uses a gentler knife-edge pop-up instead of driving all four ESCs into current limit.
  • The bulk capacitor matters for ripple, not for the surge minimum. Removing it raises the hover ripple from 150 to 237 mV pk-pk, while the surge minimum only moves to 10.94 V. The capacitor carries 2.4 A rms at hover and 7.8 A rms at full current, which sets its ripple-current rating.

ESC power stage and motor

esc_bldc.cir
  • Bus current at hover matches; the duty needed does not. SPICE draws 4.85 A from the bus against the model's 4.81 A, but needs 59.3 % duty where the model expected 55.6 %. Without timing advance the gap is far larger: at 11.0k rpm little voltage is left after back-EMF, and the winding inductance cannot build current within one commutation step.
  • Duty is a steep control input. Back-EMF cancels most of the applied voltage, so one percent of duty moves shaft power by roughly ten watts. That is why the flight controller needs rotor-speed feedback and battery-voltage compensation.
  • Ripple is large. Each phase carries 7.07 A rms at hover with 24.24 A pk-pk ripple at 48 kHz, so motor iron and copper see far more than the average current suggests.
  • The knife-edge pop-up is right at the limit. It needs about 126 W per rotor at 13.4k rpm. At 98 % duty on a sagged 14.2 V bus one ESC delivers 254 W (20.5 A from the bus, 87.1 % efficient), so the manoeuvre only works while the pack can hold its voltage.

12 V rail for the companion computer

buck12.cir
  • The rail regulates at 12.149 V with 29 mV pk-pk ripple and 0.64 A pk-pk inductor ripple; through the 4.5 W load step it stays above 12.040 V.
  • Dropout starts at 12.32 V and the computer resets at 11.77 V on the pack. Once the duty saturates at 95 %, the rail tracks the pack minus the switch and inductor losses. This is the margin the take-off and pop-up surges eat into.
